How AMI Works

Clinical intelligence designed to support — not replace — medical judgement.

1. Secure clinical data intake

AMI accepts routine clinical inputs such as laboratory reports (CBC, chemistry), radiology findings, and structured clinical data. Documents can be scanned or digital.

Each report is processed in isolation — no cross-patient contamination, no model memory.

2. Robust extraction, not guesswork

AMI uses deterministic parsing and OCR validation layers to extract values exactly as they appear on the source report. Units, reference ranges, and flags are preserved.

If data is missing, ambiguous, or unclear, AMI does not fabricate values.

3. Canonical clinical normalization

Extracted values are normalized into a structured clinical schema (e.g. haemoglobin, MCV, platelets, creatinine, CRP).

This allows consistent interpretation regardless of lab format or provider.

4. Pattern recognition — not diagnosis

AMI evaluates patterns across results:

  • Inflammatory and infectious signatures
  • Cytopenias and marrow stress patterns
  • Renal and electrolyte derangements
  • High-risk physiological combinations

AMI does not make diagnoses. It highlights clinically relevant patterns that warrant attention.

5. Explainable clinical reasoning

Every flagged finding is accompanied by plain-language clinical reasoning:

  • Why this matters physiologically
  • What systems may be involved
  • What clinicians typically verify next

6. Severity & urgency stratification

AMI classifies overall severity using clear textual categories: normal, mild, moderate, severe, critical.

Urgency flags help clinicians prioritise attention — especially in busy ER or after-hours settings.

7. Clinician remains in control

AMI never replaces clinical judgement. It does not issue treatment orders, diagnoses, or admission decisions.

The clinician remains fully responsible — AMI exists to reduce cognitive load, missed patterns, and time to insight.
