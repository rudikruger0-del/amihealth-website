Where AMI Fits Into Clinical Practice

Designed for real workflows, real pressure, and real patients.

Emergency Departments

In emergency settings, clinicians must rapidly triage incomplete, fragmented, and high-risk data.

  • Rapid prioritisation of abnormal CBC and chemistry results
  • Early identification of multisystem derangement
  • Highlighting combinations that increase risk of deterioration
  • Supporting handovers and senior escalation decisions

AMI does not replace clinical assessment — it accelerates recognition of patterns that matter.

After-Hours & On-Call Practice

When reviewing results remotely or after hours, context is often limited.

  • Clear, structured summaries of abnormal findings
  • Reduced cognitive load when fatigued or time-pressured
  • Consistency across repeated result reviews

AMI acts as a second set of eyes — not a decision-maker.

General Practice

In primary care, subtle abnormalities and evolving trends are easy to miss across multiple patients.

  • Pattern recognition across CBC and chemistry
  • Early flagging of inflammatory, renal, or haematological stress
  • Improved confidence when reviewing routine investigations

This supports earlier follow-up and better-informed consultations.

Hospital Wards

Ward clinicians often review large volumes of laboratory data across multiple patients.

  • Structured summaries reduce review time
  • Consistency across junior and senior staff
  • Improved continuity during shift changes

Radiology & Multidisciplinary Context

Imaging findings do not exist in isolation.

  • Laboratory context supports imaging interpretation
  • Inflammatory or metabolic patterns provide clinical background
  • More informed MDT discussions

High-Volume Laboratory Review

For clinicians reviewing large batches of results:

  • Faster identification of outliers
  • Reduced risk of oversight
  • Consistent interpretation across time

What AMI is not used for

  • Making diagnoses
  • Prescribing or recommending treatment
  • Replacing clinical judgement
  • Automating admission or discharge decisions

AMI exists to support thinking — not to think instead of clinicians.
